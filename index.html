Why dictation works

Writing what you hear makes you notice what you don't know yet

Dictation is an old classroom technique. Research on memory and on language learning points to three reasons it is worth your time.

It makes you recall, not just recognize

Reading or listening again feels productive, but pulling the answer out of memory builds stronger memories. In Roediger and Karpicke's experiments, students who practiced recalling a passage remembered more a week later than students who simply restudied it.

Roediger & Karpicke (2006), Perspectives on Psychological Science. Known as the testing effect.

It shows you the gap

Second-language researchers argue that producing language, rather than only taking it in, makes learners notice the difference between what they can say and what they need to say. A wrong word in your sentence is that gap, made visible.

Swain (1995), the output hypothesis. Dictation-style reconstruction tasks prompted noticing in Kowal & Swain's classroom study.

It trains ear, spelling and grammar together

To write a sentence correctly you must catch the sounds, parse the words and spell and inflect them. Small classroom studies of regular dictation practice report gains in listening comprehension for second-language learners.

Evidence here is smaller and more mixed: studies are small and results vary by measure.

An honest note: the strongest evidence is for retrieval practice in general. Studies of dictation itself are fewer and smaller. Echo Quillo is built on these ideas, not on a claim that any app guarantees fluency.

Private by design

Your practice stays yours

Echo Quillo is built on Apple's on-device speech, text and language technology, so you can practice without an account. Text generation, translation, photo text and audio transcription all run on your device.

Progress tracking. See how your practice adds up over time.
Pick up where you left off. Continue your latest practice in one tap.
Light, dark and cool themes. A warm, calm look that's easy on the eyes.
Works with your files. Choose a folder, including iCloud Drive, to keep your sessions.

About speech scoring. Pronunciation scoring runs on your device when your language is supported there. For other languages you can choose to turn on Apple's speech service in Settings. It is off by default, and when it is on, those recordings are sent to Apple for recognition under Apple's privacy policy. When it is off, they never leave your device. Read the privacy policy.
